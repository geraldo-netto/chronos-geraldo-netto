The finding itself was verified and is not disputed: `ClocksList.__init__` calls `normalize_clock_setting(info, key, settings)` before `JSONSettingsList.__init__` (`chronos_settings_widgets_worldclocks.py:587`), which writes back through `settings.set_value(key, normalized)` whenever the projection differs (`:279-289`); `normalize_saved_clocks` (`:249-276`) drops non-dict rows, rows whose `label` is blank after `strip()` even with a valid `timezone`, rows naming a runtime built-in, and — via `break` at `:273-274` — everything past the eighth entry; and because Cinnamon builds every settings page up front, merely opening the window discards those rows with no prompt and no undo, while the JS runtime filters the same rows non-destructively at render time (`worldclockData.js:453-461,463-487`). | T740 | Deferred | Publish 2028+ dates for the religious observance tables that are not Hebrew-calendar backed. | The Hebrew keys no longer need a table at all - `hebrewCalendar.js` computes them exactly for any year - and the Baha'i keys were corrected and now run 1844-2065: the pre-2015 Western convention (fixed 21 March) for 1844-2014, the Baha'i World Centre's published astronomical table for 2015-2065, and Ridvan derived as Naw-Ruz + 31 rather than tabulated. Past 2065 nothing authoritative exists - and 2092 and 2096 are 19 March on the best available reckoning, so the 20/21 alternation cannot simply be extrapolated. The remaining keys stay bounded, and computing them was evaluated and rejected on precision. **Islam**: tabular Hijri is closed-form but matches Umm al-Qura on only 55% of `eid-al-fitr` and 59-74% of the other keys over AH 1300-1523, and Umm al-Qura is itself tabulated data whose criterion changed four times, is absent before 1883, and is explicitly civil-only - its makers note first sighting can trail it by two days, and Eid al-Fitr 2025 was kept on two different days by 11 and 15 countries respectively. **Baha'i**: Naw-Ruz 2026 turns on about a minute between the equinox and Tehran sunset, and Tehran's 1189 m elevation alone moves that sunset five minutes; the definition changed in 2015 and the authoritative table stops at 2065. **Hinduism, Jainism, Buddhism, Sikhism**: there is no single correct civil date to compute - Diwali 2025 split Indian state governments between 20 and 21 October on local sunset, Vesak 2023 spanned 57 days across four rules, and Sikh reckoning changed in 2003 and was partly reverted in 2010. **Chinese**: computable but out of scope here; see T776. Unparks when the cited multi-faith calendars publish past 2027, or when a per-tradition authority is adopted and the convention recorded per key. See T774 and T775 for data defects found while checking. The suite gate no longer collides with this: it fails only once the tables cannot answer for the current year, and warns for a year before that, naming the religions that are about to lapse. |

## Recorded decisions

| id | decision |
|----|----------|
| D01 | Keep the third-party endpoints rather than a bundled data set or a single vendor. A bundled table goes stale and covers fewer countries; a single provider breaks whenever it is down — the fallback chain exists because the primary is a hobbyist service. Risk accepted with mitigations (weather opt-in; one-time local-tzdata holiday default with visible third-party disclosure and explicit disable/override; size caps; bounded spans; shape checks in and out of cache; colour filtering; no URL logging). |
| D02 | **Keep the widened export surface.** ~80 exported names have no cross-module consumer — used inside their own file and otherwise only by `test/`. The applet runs inside a compositor where a bug costs the whole session; testing only through the composition root would be slow and flaky. **This does not license the inverse:** production must not bend to a test double's shape. |
| D03 | **`author` is the display name "Geraldo Netto", not the GitHub handle.** `info.json`/`package.json` use "Geraldo Netto"; `schema_static.test.js` asserts it. The Spices convention prefers the handle, but the display name is the maintainer's explicit choice. |
