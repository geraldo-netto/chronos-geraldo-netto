| T775 | Won't fix | `guru-gobind-singh-jayanti` cannot be represented by one date per year, and the applet has not chosen a reckoning. | **[declined by the maintainer 2026-08-06]** Two calendars are in active use and they disagree. Under Bikrami reckoning the anniversary is Poh Sudi 7, which falls twice in some Gregorian years and not at all in others - 9 January 2022 and 29 December 2022, leaving 2023 with none, then 17 January 2024 - and the table holds one cell per year. The SGPC adopted Purewal's tropical Nanakshahi calendar in 2003, moved the month starts back onto Bikrami in 2010, and by 2014 had reverted while keeping the name; the 1999/2003 version was rebranded Mool Nanakshahi in 2017 and is what the PSGPC and most diaspora gurdwaras follow. | T776 | Won't fix | The Chinese folk observances are computable, but only with an in-tree astronomical implementation and only for part of the range. | **[declined by the maintainer 2026-08-06]** The rule is fully specified by GB/T 33661-2017 (Purple Mountain Observatory) - a month starts on the day at 120 deg E containing the new moon, month 11 contains the December solstice, and the leap month is the first zhongqi-less month between consecutive month-11s - so `chinese-new-year` and `ghost-festival` are computable and `qingming` needs only solar longitude 15 deg. What is declined is building and carrying the ephemeris to do it. Three findings stand and are kept so no later audit re-derives them. **`Intl`/ICU is not a usable shortcut**: its Chinese calendar disagrees with a validated Meeus ch.49 implementation in 6 of 301 years (1808, 1814, 1916, 1954, 2027, 2030), and the Hong Kong Observatory open data confirms ICU is the wrong one for 2027 - the new moon is 2027-02-06 23:56 CST, four minutes from midnight, and ICU says 7 February. **The range would not be 1800-2100**: before 1929 the calendar used Beijing apparent solar time at 116d25'E, about 14 minutes off 120 deg E plus the equation of time, moving roughly 18 of 1595 pre-1929 new moons to a different civil date. **Two lunations are undecidable at any precision** - 2057-09-28 23:59:57 CST and 2097-08-07 23:59:39 CST - because delta-T past 2050 is not knowable to seconds; also the "second new moon after the solstice" shortcut breaks in 2033 for the first time since the 1645 reform. Unpark by deciding to carry an ephemeris in-tree and a bounded range with it; ground truth for a test is the HK Observatory conversion tables, 1901-2100. See T740, which parks the rest of the non-Hebrew observance tables. |

## Recorded decisions

| id | decision |
|----|----------|
| D01 | Keep the third-party endpoints rather than a bundled data set or a single vendor. A bundled table goes stale and covers fewer countries; a single provider breaks whenever it is down — the fallback chain exists because the primary is a hobbyist service. Risk accepted with mitigations (weather opt-in; one-time local-tzdata holiday default with visible third-party disclosure and explicit disable/override; size caps; bounded spans; shape checks in and out of cache; colour filtering; no URL logging). |
| D02 | **Keep the widened export surface.** ~80 exported names have no cross-module consumer — used inside their own file and otherwise only by `test/`. The applet runs inside a compositor where a bug costs the whole session; testing only through the composition root would be slow and flaky. **This does not license the inverse:** production must not bend to a test double's shape. |
| D03 | **`author` is the display name "Geraldo Netto", not the GitHub handle.** `info.json`/`package.json` use "Geraldo Netto"; `schema_static.test.js` asserts it. The Spices convention prefers the handle, but the display name is the maintainer's explicit choice. |
